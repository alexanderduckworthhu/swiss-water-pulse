Every river, every day of 2025

How to read the picture

Each river is a ribbon. The height follows that day’s discharge, on one scale for the whole country, so the Rhine is wide and an alpine stream is a thread. The colour is the same reach against its own normal for 2025: brown when it is about a tenth of usual, cream when it is usual, deep blue when it is about ten times usual.

The number under the date is water leaving Switzerland. It is the sum of five outlets: the Rhine at Basel, the Rhône at Chancy, the Ticino toward Lago Maggiore, the Inn toward Austria, and the Doubs at Ocourt. It is not a closed water balance. Rain, snow, lakes, and the water that stays in the country are not in that sum.

The line at the lower right is that same sum through the year, on a log scale. The part already played is solid. The rest of the year is faint. The dot is the day on screen.

The page

The film runs at three frames a day, so a day eases into the next instead of jumping. The slider and the calendar both move to a calendar day. Play runs the year from wherever you are. The chat answers from the same daily series and can open the day it is talking about.

Where the numbers come from

Daily discharge is GloFAS v4 historical, the Copernicus Emergency Management Service field for average river discharge over the previous 24 hours. Each reach is snapped to a GloFAS cell at its downstream end. A cell is kept when its upstream area is within a factor of two of the river’s. Otherwise the discharge is scaled from the next matched reach downstream. About 97% of the drawn reaches matched a cell.

The rivers on the page are HydroRIVERS. Lakes are swissTLMRegio, glaciers are the Swiss Glacier Inventory 2016, the border is swissBOUNDARIES3D, and the hillshade is Mapzen terrain tiles. The normal for the colour is each reach’s own median across 2025, not a 1991–2020 climatology.

What the picture cannot say

GloFAS is a grid of about five kilometres. In the Alps a headwater often fails the area test and inherits a scaled value from downstream, so a local storm that never reaches that cell is missing. Lakes are regulated. The Aare below Thun and Brienz, the Rhône below Geneva, and the Rhine below Constance will not match a natural gauge even on a good day. Lake colour stays on the usual blue until lake-level series are added. There is no precipitation wash on this cut.
